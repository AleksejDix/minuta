A `Date` knows one calendar, the Gregorian. Temporal knows many, and every
Temporal date carries its calendar. minuta follows it: a month is the
calendar's month, a year the calendar's year, a quarter three of its months.

## Pick a calendar

There are two ways, and both give the same periods:

```ts
import { period } from "@aleksejdix/minuta";
import { createUnits, withUnits } from "@aleksejdix/minuta/core";

// 1. Pass a date in that calendar: periods keep it
period(Temporal.PlainDate.from("2026-03-18").withCalendar("hebrew"), "month");

// 2. Read every date in one calendar
const hijri = withUnits(createUnits({ calendar: "islamic-umalqura" }));
hijri.period(Temporal.PlainDate.from("2026-03-18"), "month");
// → Ramadan 1447: { start: 2026-02-18, end: 2026-03-20 } in ISO terms
```

Without `calendar`, a date keeps its own calendar, and dates made from
moments are ISO 8601, the same on every server and in every browser.

## Months and years have their own length

- A Hijri month has 29 or 30 days, a Hijri year 354 or 355.
- A Persian year starts at Nowruz, around March 21.
- A Hebrew year has 12 months, or 13 in a leap year (Adar I is added).
- A Chinese or Korean year has 13 months when it has a leap month.
- Ethiopic and Coptic years always have 13 months: twelve of 30 days and
  Pagume, 5 or 6 days.

`duration(year, "month")`, `divide(year, "month")` and the grids count what
the calendar has.

## Quarters in years with 13 months

Quarters are a business idea, and most places do business in years of 12
months: Israel's and Saudi Arabia's budgets follow the Gregorian year, and
Iran's fiscal year is the Persian year, 12 months from Nowruz. Ethiopia's fiscal year starts on Hamle 1 (July
8), so the short 13th month, Pagume, sits inside a quarter rather than
standing alone.

minuta does the same in every calendar: **a year always has four quarters
of three months**, and an extra month joins a quarter instead of making a
fifth.

- A **leap month** joins the quarter of the month it repeats: Hebrew Adar I
  joins the second quarter, a Chinese leap sixth month too.
- A **13th month** (Ethiopic and Coptic Pagume) joins the fourth quarter.

```ts
const hebrew = withUnits(createUnits({ calendar: "hebrew" }));
const quarters = hebrew.divide(
  hebrew.period(Temporal.PlainDate.from("2024-01-15"), "year"),
  "quarter"
);
quarters.map((quarter) => formatRange(quarter, "en-US", { month: "long" }));
// → ["Tishri – Kislev 5784", "Tevet – Adar II 5784", "Nisan – Sivan 5784", "Tamuz – Elul 5784"]
```

## Japanese eras

A Japanese year is the whole year, even when an era starts in it. 2019 runs
from January 1 to December 31, from Heisei 31 into Reiwa 1:

```ts
const year = period(
  Temporal.PlainDate.from("2019-06-01").withCalendar("japanese"),
  "year"
);
formatRange(year, "ja-JP"); // → "平成31年～令和元年"
```

## Labels

[`formatPeriod`](../../api/minuta-format/format-period/#other-calendars) and
`formatRange` show a period in its calendar: "Adar I 5784",
"Ramadan 1447 AH". Pass `calendar` in the options to label it in another one.

## Weeks stay weeks

A week is seven days in every calendar, so the week start and the weekend
come from [the units](../date-engines/), not from the calendar:
`createUnits({ calendar: "islamic-umalqura", weekStartsOn: "saturday", weekend: ["friday"] })`.

## Where they run

| Runtime                                      | Calendars                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Chrome and Edge 144+, Firefox 139+, Node 26+ | all, with Temporal built in                                                             |
| Safari, Node 24 (the polyfill)               | all but Chinese, Korean, Ethiopic and Coptic: `@js-temporal/polyfill` 0.5 fails on them |

The Julian calendar is not part of Temporal, so it is not here yet.
